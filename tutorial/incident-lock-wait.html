An order that waits

An order takes 14 seconds, nothing fails, and the slow query updates one row. The trace shows where the time went. Only Postgres can say what it was waiting for.

1Trigger it

lock_wait places two orders, one second apart. The first keeps its row locked while it waits for a slow payment provider.

2Symptom

The request succeeds, but it took 15.4 seconds. The response says the second order took 14.2 of them. Nothing failed, and no span has an error.

3What the trace shows

The trace is right about where the time went, but it can't say why. It looks just like a slow query. From this alone you could blame the SQL, a missing index or the disk, and you'd be wrong each time.

4What Postgres shows

Postgres logs any wait longer than deadlock_timeout, which is 1 second by default:

Loading saved query...

That's the whole answer: this statement isn't slow, it's waiting for another session. The count of blocked sessions shows the same thing on a chart:

Loading saved query...

While it's happening, you can also ask Postgres directly who is blocking whom:

Be careful with the metric you'd reach for first. postgresql.database.locks never showed this wait, as the start of this lab explains.

5Root cause

An UPDATE locks its row until the transaction ends, and here the transaction stays open during a slow call. Everyone who wants that row queues up behind it. In a real app that might be a payment provider, an email or any other HTTP call.
