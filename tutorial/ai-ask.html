It used the events to rule out a probe failure and a rollout. kubectl was blocked by a rule we've since fixed, so it says what it couldn't check.

When it wasn't what we expected

We asked again later, after starting the same leak. This time the machine was overloaded, which we hadn't noticed, because we'd left three load generators running on Docker's 4 CPUs. So the OOM kill we expected never happened. 156 seconds, 7 turns, $0.11:

It was right, and we were wrong. We checked what it said against what happened:

WhereWhat it shows
The exit Kubernetes recordedexitCode: 137, reason: Error, not OOMKilled
The Kubernetes event, at 14:22:11Container workload failed liveness probe, will be restarted
The kernel log, 14:19 to 14:26No out of memory lines at all
The app's last log lineholding 192 MiB on purpose, of a 256 MiB limit

The liveness probe timed out on the starved machine, and the pod was killed with memory to spare. An answer that followed the obvious story would have said OOM.

It also shows what the agent can't do when the platform is struggling. Three of its SigNoz queries timed out, because ClickHouse was starved too. It said so, and it lowered its confidence. Don't rely on an agent that reads SigNoz to explain SigNoz being down.

Every number matches. And it answered the second question better than it was asked: no product is really slower, and the statistics disagree because of noise. A dashboard would have shown you a slowest product anyway.

Check that the time range is over. Our first attempts asked about 13:00 to 13:10 while it was still 13:06. Its numbers looked 7% short of ours, because each of us counted an unfinished window at a different moment. The mistake was ours, not the agent's, but neither of us noticed it from the answer alone.
